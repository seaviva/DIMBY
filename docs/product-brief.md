# Product Brief (Interview Draft)

## Vision
Create a web experience that visualizes NYC real estate valuations as a heat map, enabling users to explore valuation intensity across neighborhoods.

## Interview Answers (recorded)
### Goals & Audience
1. Primary audience: the requester (personal research project, possibly broader later).
2. Single most important outcome: a functioning webpage showing a detailed NYC real estate valuation heat map.
3. Secondary audiences: none for now; future features may add broader appeal (e.g., toggles, trend analysis).

### Data & Licensing
4. Data sources: prefer free sources; web scraping is acceptable if needed.
5. Licensing/attribution: none known yet.
6. Update cadence: weekly updates, plus a manual refresh button.

### Map & Interactions
7. Interactions: hover tooltips for contextual details; filtering by number of rooms; possible toggles between sale valuation and rental rates.
8. Granularity: as granular as possible, ideally building-level.
9. Views: heat map only for now.

### Performance & UX
10. Performance constraints: none specified; web app is sufficient.
11. Accessibility: no explicit requirements.
12. Visual style: clean, aesthetically sharp.

### Delivery & Ops
13. Tech stack: no preference; choose what’s optimal.
14. Hosting: no preference; recommend what fits best for a new developer.
15. Analytics: not needed for now.

## Initial Assumptions (confirmed)
- Public, web-first experience.
- Heat map as primary visualization.
- Data pipeline required to transform raw valuation data into spatial aggregates.

## Open Research Items
- Identify best free NYC real estate valuation datasets (public data portals, assessor data, etc.).
- Identify best free NYC rental rate datasets (if different from sale valuation sources).
- Determine whether building-level coordinates and valuations are readily available or require geocoding.
- Confirm licensing terms and attribution for any chosen data sources.
